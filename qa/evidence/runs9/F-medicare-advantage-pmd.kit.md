**Type of denial:** Medical necessity — the denial states the face-to-face visit note "does not describe why a walker or a manual wheelchair is insufficient for your MRADLs" and "the supplier's home assessment was not included with the request."

**Plan:** Medicare Advantage (the notice names "Silverline Complete (HMO), a Medicare Advantage plan"). You, for your father — a representative form comes first.

**Urgent:** No — standard review (unless your doctor says waiting could seriously harm your health), because the equipment has not yet been received and the source does not report that the condition is worsening or that your father's doctor has said waiting could seriously harm his health.

**What they're really saying:** The plan requires documentation that a walker and a manual wheelchair are both insufficient — specifically, why your father cannot walk with the walker for his activities of daily living in the home and why he cannot propel a manual wheelchair. The supplier's home assessment, which was done, was not sent with the request.

**What they have to show:** Medicare's coverage rules for power mobility devices require the medical records to show "a mobility limitation that significantly impairs your ability to perform one or more mobility-related activities of daily living (MRADLs) such as toileting, feeding, dressing, grooming, and bathing in your home; that the limitation cannot be sufficiently resolved by a cane or walker; that you do not have sufficient upper-extremity function to propel an optimally configured manual wheelchair; that you can safely operate a power wheelchair; and that a face-to-face mobility examination was completed."

**Your strongest argument:** The face-to-face visit did occur, and the patient reports he can walk only a few steps with the walker before becoming too short of breath to continue, resulting in incontinence accidents when he cannot reach the bathroom in time, and that his shoulder problems prevent him from propelling a manual wheelchair. The home assessment was completed but not submitted.

**Case strength:** Good — one thing missing. The clinical facts that make a walker and manual wheelchair insufficient are documented or reported, but the supplier's home assessment and possibly a more detailed description of the limitations in the doctor's note were not included with the request.

**What would make it decisive:**
- The supplier's home assessment from September 30, 2026
- A letter or updated visit note from Dr. Okoro documenting the specific functional limitations — that your father becomes severely short of breath after only a few steps with the walker, cannot complete MRADLs (specifically toileting) in time, and cannot propel a manual wheelchair due to shoulder impairment — and stating that these limitations require a power wheelchair
- Documentation of safe operation of a power wheelchair (often observed during the home assessment or a trial)
- The oxygen prescription and COPD severity measures, if available

**Who fixes this:** Great Lakes Mobility must send the missing home assessment to the plan with a corrected request. Dr. Okoro's office should provide a letter or updated note documenting why the walker and manual wheelchair are insufficient for your father's MRADLs. You file the appeal by the deadline with these documents enclosed.

**What you may actually owe:** The denial is of coverage for the equipment before it is provided, so you would owe the full cost if you obtained it without coverage; do not purchase or rent the wheelchair until the appeal is decided or you confirm what you would owe.

**Do this first:** Complete the Medicare Appointment of Representative form (CMS-1696) or send a power of attorney so you can file and track the appeal for your father.
