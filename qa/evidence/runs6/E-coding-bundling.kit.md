**Type of denial:** Coding and bundling — lines 2, 3, and 4 denied as "The benefit for this service is included in the payment/allowance for another service/procedure that has already been adjudicated" (CO-97) and "Separately billed services/tests have been bundled as they are considered components of the same procedure" (M15); line 3 also denied as "The procedure code is inconsistent with the modifier used, or a required modifier is missing" (CO-4).

**Plan:** Self-funded employer plan (the notice states "This plan is self-funded and is governed by ERISA"); you are filing for yourself.

**Urgent:** No — standard review (the care was already received).

**What they're really saying:** The insurer paid for the injection procedure itself (code 20610) but bundled the medication, the ultrasound guidance, and the office visit into that procedure payment — they are treating these as included components that should not be billed separately. The visit code was also denied because modifier 25 (which tells the insurer the visit was separate and significant) may have been used incorrectly, or the documentation may not support a separately billable visit.

**What they have to show:** The insurer applied claim-edit rules that bundle certain services together — the notice says you may request "the plan provisions and claim-edit rules used, free of charge."

**Your strongest argument:** The office visit addressed your shoulder, which is a separate problem from the knee injection — when a visit on the same day addresses a distinct problem, it is separately reportable with modifier 25, and the diagnosis codes on the claim should reflect that. The medication (triamcinolone) is a separately payable drug when injected into a joint, and ultrasound guidance (76942) is separately reportable when the physician uses real-time imaging to guide the needle — whether these were done and correctly coded is a question for the billing office to verify against the procedure note.

**Case strength:** Good — one thing missing. The billing office must verify that the codes match what was actually done and that the visit's documentation supports a separate, significant evaluation of the shoulder with the correct diagnosis codes.

**What would make it decisive:**
- The procedure note from 08/21/2026 showing whether ultrasound guidance was used in real time during the injection
- The office-visit note from 08/21/2026 documenting the separate evaluation of the shoulder, with the shoulder diagnosis
- Corrected claim with the shoulder diagnosis code on line 3 (the visit) and verification that modifier 25 is supported by documentation of a separately identifiable service
- Confirmation from the billing office that J3301 x4 units and 76942 are correctly coded for what was done

**Who fixes this:** Your doctor's billing office must verify the codes against the procedure note and visit note — including whether ultrasound guidance was used, whether the visit addressed a separate problem with a separate diagnosis, and whether the claim should be corrected and resubmitted.

**What you may actually owe:** The "CO" (contractual obligation) reason codes mean these are the provider's write-off under the contract with the plan, not amounts you owe — for an in-network provider, you should owe only the $22.48 patient responsibility shown on line 1. If the billing office sent you a bill for $668.48, do not pay the disputed $646 (lines 2, 3, and 4) until the claim is corrected.

**Do this first:** Call Chesapeake Orthopaedic Associates' billing office and ask them to review the claim against the visit and procedure notes, verify the codes, and submit a corrected claim if needed — and ask them to hold your account and not send it to collections while this is corrected.
