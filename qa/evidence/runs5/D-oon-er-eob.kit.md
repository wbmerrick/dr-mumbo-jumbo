**What they're really saying:** The plan denied the claim because it classified your ER visit as non-emergency care and the emergency physician is out of network. Under an HMO, out-of-network care is generally not covered except when it is emergency care — so the entire fight is whether the visit met the emergency standard, which federal law judges by your symptoms when you went in, not the final diagnosis.

**What they have to show:** The plan's definition of emergency services from the Evidence of Coverage, and the basis for determining this visit was not an emergency. Federal surprise-billing rules define emergency services by the prudent-layperson standard: whether a person with average knowledge of medicine would reasonably believe that without immediate medical attention, the symptoms could result in serious jeopardy to health, serious impairment of bodily functions, or serious dysfunction of a bodily organ or part.

**Your strongest argument:** Crushing chest pain and shortness of breath at 2 a.m. meet the prudent-layperson emergency standard — a reasonable person would believe these symptoms could indicate a heart attack or other life-threatening condition requiring immediate evaluation, and the ER workup (EKG, troponin, chest x-ray, six-hour stay) confirms the treating physicians took the complaint seriously as a potential emergency.

**Case strength:** Strong. Federal rules protect emergency care regardless of final diagnosis, the facility was in-network, and you had no choice of which emergency physician staffed that ER.

**What would make it decisive:**
- The ER records showing your presenting symptoms, vital signs, and the time you arrived
- Documentation that Cascade Emergency Physicians staffs Mercy Lakeside's ER (making them ancillary out-of-network clinicians under the No Surprises Act)
- Confirmation that Mercy Lakeside Hospital is a participating facility in your plan

**Who fixes this:** You file the appeal. The appeal asks the plan to reprocess the claim as emergency care under federal surprise-billing protections, which means coverage at in-network cost-sharing and no balance billing by the out-of-network physician. Cascade Emergency Physicians may not bill you for the denied amount while this is in dispute or after — report continued balance billing to the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.

**What you may actually owe:** Under federal surprise-billing rules, if the visit is reprocessed as emergency care, you owe only your plan's in-network cost-sharing (your HMO copay for an ER visit, which is usually a fixed amount like $100–$350). The physician cannot balance bill you for the difference between their charge and what the plan pays. If the plan continues to deny the claim as non-emergency, that decision can go to external review because it is a medical judgment about whether your symptoms were an emergency.

**Do this first:** File the appeal by the 180-day deadline (April 4, 2027) asking the plan to reprocess the claim as emergency care; call Cascade Emergency Physicians to confirm they will hold the account while the appeal is pending, and report the balance bill to the No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.
