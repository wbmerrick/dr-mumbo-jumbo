## 5. Peer-to-peer talking points — for your doctor

**Before the call:**
- Have the chart open to the patient's current heart-failure status ([NYHA class], [ejection fraction], current medications — furosemide documented), your order for attended polysomnography, and the reported symptoms (loud snoring, witnessed apneas, excessive daytime sleepiness, fell asleep while driving).

- Request a copy of the insurer's clinical policy for sleep studies showing the criteria for attended polysomnography versus home sleep apnea testing — ask for the exact policy name and number.
- At the start of the call, ask the reviewer's name, credentials, and specialty.

**30-second opening:**
"This is Dr. [name], [specialty], calling about [patient name, date of birth]. [I / Dr. ___] ordered attended polysomnography for suspected obstructive sleep apnea in a patient with [NYHA class or ejection fraction] congestive heart failure on diuretic therapy. The patient reports loud snoring, witnessed apneas by the spouse, severe daytime sleepiness, and fell asleep at a red light. Home sleep apnea testing is not recommended for this patient because it may miss central or complex sleep apnea and does not monitor cardiac rhythm or oxygen desaturation patterns that are needed to guide heart-failure management."

**Criteria, point by point:**
- **"Home sleep apnea testing is appropriate for uncomplicated patients":** This patient is complicated by [NYHA class or ejection fraction] congestive heart failure. Most insurers' policies for polysomnography list moderate-to-severe cardiopulmonary disease as an indication for attended testing rather than home testing. Home testing is not validated in heart-failure patients because central sleep apnea and complex sleep apnea are common in this population and home devices may miss them.
- **Medical necessity for any sleep study:** The patient reports witnessed apneas, loud snoring, and excessive daytime sleepiness severe enough to cause a drowsy-driving episode — [sleepiness score, if documented]. These symptoms meet criteria for sleep apnea evaluation.

**If they push back:**
- *"Try home testing first"*: Home testing may miss central apneas and Cheyne-Stokes respiration, which are prevalent in heart-failure patients and change treatment — PAP settings and heart-failure management depend on the apnea type and desaturation patterns that home testing does not capture. The guideline recommendation for heart-failure patients is attended polysomnography.
- *"The policy requires documentation of heart-failure severity"*: [If documented in the chart: NYHA class is ___, ejection fraction is ___, patient is on diuretic therapy.] If severity is not yet documented in a chart note, I can provide an addendum or see the patient for updated documentation — what specific severity measure does the policy require?

**The ask:**
Overturn the denial and authorize attended polysomnography now. If you cannot approve it, please provide in writing the specific criterion you consider unmet, your name and specialty, and what documentation would change the decision.

**After the call:**
Log the date, time, reviewer name and credentials, reference number given, outcome (approved, still denied, pending documentation), and any documentation requested.
