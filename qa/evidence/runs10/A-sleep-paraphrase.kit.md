## 6. Phone script — calling the insurer

**Before you call, have ready:**
- Your denial letter with the notice date, claim/reference number, and member ID
- The date(s) of service (the date the study was ordered or scheduled)
- A pen and paper for notes
- The original order or referral for the sleep study, if you have it

**Say:** "I'm calling about a denied claim. My member ID is [from your letter], and the claim or reference number is [from your letter]. The notice is dated [from your letter]. The service is [attended polysomnography or the exact description from your letter]. Can you tell me the reason code for the denial and what it means in plain English?"

**Write down:** the reason code and the explanation.

**Then ask:** "What is the name and number of the specific medical policy or coverage criteria that was applied? Can you send me a copy of that policy?" Write down the policy name and number, and ask where it will be sent or how to access it.

**Then ask:** "Can you give me the name, credentials, and specialty of the medical reviewer who made this decision?" Write it down.

**Then ask:** "Is a peer-to-peer review available between my doctor and your medical director? If so, how does my doctor request it?" Write down the process and any phone number.

**Then ask:** "What is my exact deadline to file an appeal, and where do I send it — fax, portal, or mailing address? What documents must I include?" Write down the deadline date, the address or fax number, and the list of required items.

**Then ask:** "I want to confirm: is this plan self-funded or fully insured? If I need to file a complaint, does it go to the U.S. Department of Labor or to my state insurance department?" Write down the answer.

**Then ask:** "Can you give me a call reference number for today's call, and your name?" Write both down.

**If they offer next steps or mention reprocessing the claim:** ask them to note the account and confirm in writing what they are doing and when you will hear back. Get the reference number for that action.
