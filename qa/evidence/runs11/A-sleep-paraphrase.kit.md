## 5. Peer-to-peer talking points — for your doctor

**Before the call:**
- Have the patient's chart open, including [heart-failure diagnosis, NYHA class or ejection fraction, hospitalization history, current medications], and the dates of [prior sleep evaluation or treatment attempts, if any].
- Have the insurer's medical policy for sleep studies (request it if not provided with the denial) and the claim/reference number [from the denial letter].
- Ask the reviewer's name, credentials, and specialty at the start of the call, and ask whether they are board-certified in sleep medicine or cardiology.
- Log the call reference number.

**30-second opening:**
"I'm calling about [patient name, date of birth], for whom I ordered attended polysomnography. The patient reports loud snoring, witnessed apneas, and severe daytime sleepiness — [he/she] fell asleep at a red light. [He/She] has congestive heart failure and is currently treated with furosemide. The in-lab study is medically necessary because home sleep apnea testing is not recommended for patients with heart failure — it may miss central or complex sleep apnea patterns and does not provide the continuous cardiopulmonary monitoring needed to guide treatment in this patient."

**Criteria, point by point:**
- *Symptoms of obstructive sleep apnea:* The patient reports witnessed apneas, loud snoring, and severe daytime sleepiness with a near-miss motor-vehicle incident — [Epworth Sleepiness Scale score, if documented].
- *"Uncomplicated" patient appropriate for home testing:* The patient does not meet that criterion. [He/She] has congestive heart failure [NYHA class ___; ejection fraction ___%, if documented], a cardiac comorbidity for which home sleep apnea testing is not validated. [Cite: American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline stating that home sleep apnea testing is not recommended for patients with significant cardiopulmonary disease.]
- *Why the approved alternative is not suitable:* Home sleep apnea testing does not monitor ECG, may not reliably differentiate obstructive from central apneas, and may underestimate the apnea-hypopnea index in heart-failure patients who have frequent arousals or central events. The in-lab study will determine whether [he/she] has obstructive, central, or complex sleep apnea and will guide the choice of therapy — CPAP versus adaptive servo-ventilation versus other modalities — which differs by apnea type and has different safety profiles in heart failure.

**If they push back:**
- *"Try the home test first":* Home sleep apnea testing is not validated in heart-failure patients and may miss central apneas, leading to inappropriate therapy that could worsen the patient's heart failure. Starting with an inadequate test delays diagnosis and exposes the patient to the risk of choosing the wrong treatment.
- *"The patient can have the home test and then escalate if needed":* A false-negative home test or one that misclassifies the apnea type would delay appropriate therapy; the guideline recommends attended polysomnography as the initial test for patients with congestive heart failure.
- *Reviewer's specialty or credentials:* If the reviewer is not board-certified in sleep medicine or cardiology, ask to speak with a physician reviewer in one of those specialties.

**The ask:**
Overturn the denial and authorize attended polysomnography now. If you cannot approve it on this call, please provide in writing the specific criterion you consider unmet, your name and specialty, and what additional documentation would change the decision.

**After the call:**
Log date, time, reviewer name and credentials, call reference number, outcome, and any documentation requested. If the denial is upheld, proceed with the written appeal and ask the insurer for the peer-to-peer reviewer's decision in writing.
