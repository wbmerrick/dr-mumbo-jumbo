Privacy

What we keep, and what we don't.

Short version: nothing you paste or upload is stored, and nothing is emailed unless you ask. Here is the long version, in plain words. Last updated November 11, 2026.

What happens to the text and files you submit

When you paste a note, bill or denial letter, or add a photo or PDF, it is sent once to the AI model (Anthropic's Claude, through its API) to produce your translation, appeal letter or Fight Pack, and the result is sent back to your browser. We do not store what you submit or what comes back, we do not log it, and we never see it. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, API inputs and outputs are not used to train its models. The one exception is if you ask us to email your kit to you; see “Emailing your kit” below.

Because of that, we cannot recover a result for you later. Copy or print anything you want to keep.

What stays in your browser

The Fight Pack saves your finished kit, with the random tag it was built under (see “Visit and use counts” below), in your own browser's storage for three days, so you can come back to it. It never leaves your device, and the "Clear it from this browser" link under the kit deletes it. The Fight Pack also keeps a short-lived unlock token in your browser so you can rebuild a kit without asking for a new one. While a tab is open, it also holds one word for how you reached the site (see “Visit and use counts” below); closing the tab erases it. We do not use cookies or advertising trackers.

What we do keep

  • Waitlist sign-ups. If you leave your email for Fight Pack launch news, we keep the address and the category you chose, nothing else, in our database. We use it only for that news. Email us to have it deleted.
  • Emailing your kit. If you ask us to email your Fight Pack to you, the kit and the address you type are handed to our email provider (Resend) to deliver that one message. Resend keeps a copy of that message — its contents and its delivery records (recipient address, subject, delivery status) — for 30 days, then deletes it; we cannot shorten that. We keep the address itself alongside the waitlist above and use it only for launch news. Email us to have it deleted. If you would rather nothing leave your browser at all, use “Copy everything” or “Print” instead.
  • Check-ins. Under “Email me my kit” there is a box you can tick. If you tick it, you are sent two more short emails: one 3 days later, asking whether your appeal has been sent, and one about four weeks later, asking what happened. They are written and handed to Resend when your kit is sent, and held by Resend until they go out; we keep no list of who asked for them. No part of your kit is in them. Each has a link for each answer. Opening a link tells us only that the check-in page was opened; what the link carries stays in your browser until you tap an answer. When you tap one, we count three things: your kit's random tag (see “Visit and use counts” below), which question it was, and which answer you gave. That is how we learn whether kits get sent, and whether appeals are won. Your answer is counted with that tag and never with your address: we do not record which address goes with which tag. Resend does hold the two together for a time, because the tag is inside the emails it sends you; it deletes each email no later than 30 days after sending it, as it does your kit. If you do not want the second email, reply to the first and say so, and we will cancel it. If you do not tick the box, none of this happens.
  • Server logs. Our hosting provider (Vercel) keeps standard, short-lived request logs (time, URL, IP address, browser type) for security and operations, as every website host does. They do not contain the text you submit.
  • Visit and use counts. We count visits and a handful of actions — a translation run, an appeal letter generated, a Fight Pack built or emailed, a check-in answered — so we can tell whether these tools are being used at all. This is done two ways: Vercel Web Analytics, which is cookieless, does not follow you to other sites and never receives what you paste or upload; and our own log lines, which record that an action happened and nothing about who did it or what was in it — plus, for a Fight Pack, two small things. One is a random throwaway tag that ties the six calls building your kit together so they are not miscounted as six kits; it is generated fresh for each kit, and we do not record whose kit it is. (If you ask for check-ins, the same tag is in their links, so that your answer is counted with your kit: see “Check-ins” above.) The other is one word for how you reached the site: the tag on the link you followed, or a name for the site that linked here, such as “reddit” or “google”. Our count keeps that word only, never the address of the page you came from. None of this can be tied back to you.
  • Fair-use counters. To keep the free tools free, each server counts requests per IP address for a day, in memory. The count is not written anywhere and disappears when the server restarts.

Payments

The Fight Pack is free at the moment. If paid options are introduced, payment will be handled by Stripe on Stripe's own pages; we will not see or store card numbers.

Leave out what isn't needed

You do not need to include names, dates of birth or ID numbers for the tools to work. Where a letter needs them, it leaves [brackets] for you to fill in privately. Please do not submit another person's records without their permission.

Children

The site is for adults handling their own, or a family member's, medical paperwork. It is not directed at children under 13.
