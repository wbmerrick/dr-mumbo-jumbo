Denial Playbook

They called it experimental.

Often coded CO-55, sometimes folded into CO-50 or CO-96. The plan is asserting that the treatment is unproven for your condition. That is a question about evidence, and federal rules make it one an independent reviewer can re-decide.

1

Get the evidence file they used

An experimental or investigational denial is a claim about the state of the medical literature. That is a claim you can test.

You, to the insurer"Claim [number] was denied as experimental or investigational. Please send me the clinical policy applied, the evidence and citations it relies on, the date it was last reviewed, and the specialty of the reviewer who made the decision."
  • Policies go stale. A guideline written before the pivotal trial reported is a document with a weak spot in it.
  • Note the reviewer’s specialty. A generalist denying a subspecialty treatment is worth raising by name.
2

Build the counter-evidence like a case file

You are not arguing that the plan is unkind. You are arguing that the treatment is no longer investigational for your indication, and the file has to look like it.

  • Regulatory status: is it FDA-approved or cleared, and for what? Approved-but-off-label is a different argument from unapproved, and a stronger one.
  • Specialty society guidelines and compendia that list it for your indication.
  • Peer-reviewed trials, with the few that match your situation pulled to the front. Two on point beat twenty that are adjacent.
  • Coverage by other major payers, including Medicare, if it exists.
3

Have the specialist write the "why this patient" letter

Evidence establishes that the treatment is real. The clinician letter establishes that it is right for you, which is the part a reviewer cannot look up.

  • What has already failed, with drugs, doses and dates.
  • Why the covered alternatives are unsuitable or exhausted in your case.
  • What the expected course looks like without it.
  • The Fight Pack drafts this for the treating clinician to correct and sign.
4

Request a reviewer who practises in the field

Ask, in the appeal itself, that the review be done by a physician who actively treats your condition, and ask for a peer-to-peer call with that person.

  • Put the request in writing so the refusal is also in writing.
  • If the plan’s own reviewer turns out to be unqualified for the question, that becomes part of the external review record.
5

External review is explicitly open to this

This matters and most people never hear it: federal rules list experimental and investigational determinations among the denials eligible for independent external review, alongside medical necessity and level of care.

  • An independent reviewer reads your evidence against theirs, and the decision binds the plan.
  • You generally have at least four months from the final internal denial; the letter states your deadline.
  • Standard review runs about 45 days, and about 72 hours when the delay would seriously jeopardise your health.
