    Straight answers

    Will my doctor actually sign it?

    Usually, yes — the hard part was never willingness, it was the 45 minutes nobody has. You hand over a letter that already answers the insurer's reason in their language; the office corrects what's wrong, adds specifics in the brackets, and signs. The kit includes a half-page note for the office explaining exactly that.

    Why not just the free letter?

    The free appeal letter is a good round one. The Fight Pack adds the document that carries the most weight (the doctor's letter), the scripts that get you the insurer's own criteria, the records request, the round-two external-review filing, and the deadlines — the parts people skip and then lose on.

    Does it work for any denial?

    It's built for medical-necessity, prior-authorization, out-of-network, and "not covered" denials from commercial, employer, and marketplace plans. Medicare and Medicaid appeals use different timelines; the kit flags that and defers to the deadline on your letter.

    What do you keep?

    Nothing. Your documents go to the AI model once to write the kit and are not stored. Stripe handles payment and your receipt. The finished kit stays in your browser only — print it or copy it somewhere safe.

    The serious bit: Dr Mumbo Jumbo is an educational self-advocacy tool. The Fight Pack is a set of drafts for you, and your clinician, to review, complete, and send yourselves. It does not provide legal, medical, or financial advice, does not file anything on your behalf, does not create a doctor–patient relationship, and makes no promises about outcomes. Appeal rights and deadlines vary by plan and state; your denial letter and plan documents control. Drafts are generated by AI and can contain mistakes — read every line before sending.
