Denial Playbook

The claim came back missing something.

CO-16 is one of the most common codes in American healthcare, and one of the least alarming. The claim was incomplete, so the insurer never decided anything. It belongs to the billing office, not to you — as long as somebody actually fixes it.

1

Read it as a to-do list, not a verdict

CO-16 means the claim arrived incomplete or with an error — a missing field, an absent attachment, an invalid identifier. The plan never reached the question of whether your care was covered.

  • Look for the remark codes printed next to it. Those name the actual missing item.
  • The CO prefix marks the amount as a contractual obligation between plan and provider. It is not meant to become your bill.
  • Nothing here says no to your care. Treat it as paperwork, and keep your own appeal powder dry.
2

Call the billing office, not the insurer

This one is fixed at the provider’s end, by resubmitting a corrected claim. Your call is to them.

You, to the billing office"The EOB for [date of service] came back CO-16 with remark code [code]. What’s missing, and when will you resubmit the corrected claim? Please don’t bill me while a corrected claim is pending — can you confirm that in writing?"
  • Get a date for the resubmission and a reference number.
  • Ask them to put a hold on your account in the meantime.
3

Watch the timely-filing clock

The real risk in a CO-16 is not the denial, it is the delay. Claims have filing deadlines, and a correction that sits on someone’s desk can age past them.

  • If it expires, the write-off is normally the provider’s to absorb — but only if you notice and say so.
  • Diarise a check two weeks out. If nothing moved, call again and note the dates.
  • Never let a claim that was never adjudicated drift toward collections unanswered.
4

If the bill lands on you anyway, push back

Occasionally the balance gets passed to the patient despite the CO prefix. That is worth one firm letter.

  • State that the claim was denied for missing information, that the code marks it a contractual obligation, and that you are asking for the corrected claim to be filed.
  • Ask them to identify, in writing, the contract term that makes the amount yours.
  • Send a copy to the plan, and keep everything.
5

Make sure the real answer actually arrives

Once the corrected claim goes in, you should get a new EOB with a real decision on it — paid, or denied for a substantive reason.

  • If that second EOB is a true denial, you are now in the ordinary playbook: decode it, call, document, appeal.
  • Your appeal deadlines run from that notice, not from the CO-16.
